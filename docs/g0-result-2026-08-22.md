# Phase 2 result — GO, with a quality caveat that reshapes the claim

1100 rows, 11 languages, n=50, Dream-7B threshold decoding vs Qwen2.5-7B
autoregressive, canvas sized from measured output, three in-language few-shot
exemplars. A100 80GB. Raw: `data/g0.jsonl`.

## Primary: GO

**k = 0.566 ± 0.110, R² = 0.746** over 11 languages. The prereg required
SE(k) ≤ 0.15 and R² ≥ 0.5, frozen before any data. Both clear.

4% for bn.

## The shape is not the one predicted

**The penalty saturates.** cost_ratio climbs from 0.69 to about 2.0 by Thai and
then stops: th 2.01, bn 2.10, te 2.06, across a fertility range that triples.

**Because mechanism B switches on at the top.** `parallel_factor` sits near 2.1
for the eight lowest-fertility languages and then roughly doubles — bn 4.47,
te 4.01. Intra-word redundancy does buy back parallelism, and it does so
precisely where the canvas cost is worst. That is the mechanism the design was
built to test, and it is real.

Slopes against log fertility:

| | slope |
|---|---|
| canvas cost (mechanism A) | +0.44 |
| parallel_factor (mechanism B) | +0.45 |
| **cost_ratio (net)** | **+0.57** |

A and B very nearly cancel. The net slope is carried by a third term the design
did not name: **Dream's output grows faster with fertility than Qwen's**
(implied slope difference +0.58 — 677 tokens at Bengali against 159 at German).
Part of that is verbosity, part is the model failing and rambling.

**Crossover at German.** Diffusion is faster than autoregressive only for
English, Chinese and Spanish. By German it is at parity, and past Thai it is
twice the cost. The advantage is not a property of the architecture; it is a
property of English and Chinese tokenising cheaply.

## K3 fires, and it changes what can be claimed

Accuracy collapses at exactly the high-fertility end: Swahili 24/32%, Thai
38/70%, Bengali 40/78%, Telugu 22/34%. Both arms degrade; Dream degrades
harder on th and bn.

So the efficiency comparison at the top of the axis is partly between systems
failing the task, and Dream's extra tokens there are partly the cost of
failing. **This cannot be reported as a pure efficiency result.**

It can be reported as what the framing already says it is: a **joint
quality–efficiency frontier that shifts with script**. At high fertility you
lose on both axes at once. Restricting to languages where both arms clear 60%
accuracy leaves fertility 1.00–1.49 and a cost_ratio range of 0.69–1.05 — the
crossover survives, the large spread does not.

Both numbers belong in the paper: the full range for the frontier claim, the
restricted range for the clean efficiency claim.
